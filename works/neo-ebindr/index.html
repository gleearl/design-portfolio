All works

Neo eBINDR

A records system that BBB staff work in all day: one screen per business, with complaints, reports and search close at hand. I designed it in Figma and coded the front end.

Timeline
2018
Scope
UI/UX design, front-end development

The record

One business, one screen.

Everything staff need about a business sits on its record: contact details and a map, the rating, accreditation, charts for reviews, leads and complaints, and the full list of complaints below. Quick actions run down the left edge.

Status

The state of a business at a glance.

Each business carries a status card: accredited, supporter, charity, pending, ineligible, revoked or former. Colour, icon and the key dates tell staff where it stands before they read a word. Urgent notices from operators run across the top of the record in red.

On the record

Work happens on the record.

Complaints, form letters and reports open as windows on top of the record, so staff never lose their place. Emails, calls, invoices and payments land on the record’s timeline, with a calendar to jump to a date.

The system

Its own colours and parts.

The product has its own small system: a dark blue, a blue and an orange accent, a set of neutrals for text and lines, and one type scale. Components were built on top, so every window looks and works the same.

Principles

Three rules behind every screen.

  1. 01

    Keep the record in view

    Tasks open as windows over the record, never as a new page, so the context stays one glance away.

  2. 02

    Dense, but ordered

    A lot of data on one screen, grouped into cards and tables with clear labels, so it can be scanned quickly.

  3. 03

    Flag what matters

    Status cards in colour and operator alerts in red, so the important things are seen first.

My role

Designed it, then built it.

Design

The record, the windows, reports, tools and settings in Figma, together with the colour, type and component set behind them.

Front end

I coded the screens myself. One set of components for cards, tables and windows kept a large product consistent.
